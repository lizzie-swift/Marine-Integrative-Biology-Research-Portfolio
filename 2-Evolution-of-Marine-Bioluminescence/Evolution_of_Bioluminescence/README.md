Brief summary of the review.

Research Aim

This review examines the evolutionary history of marine bioluminescence, with a particular focus on the evolutionary, genomic and regulatory mechanisms that have enabled light-producing systems to emerge repeatedly across diverse marine lineages.

<img width="4032" height="2078" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ff9a827f-e1d4-4639-b10f-d941ab176360" />


Bioluminescence is the production and emission of visible light by living organisms through specialised biochemical reactions involving molecules known as luciferins and enzymes called luciferases (Schramm & Weiß, 2024).

The widespread occurrence of bioluminescence across diverse marine taxa suggests that light-producing systems have evolved repeatedly throughout evolutionary history. Understanding how these systems arose provides an opportunity to investigate the roles of genome evolution, genetic innovation and gene regulation in the emergence of complex biological traits (Haddock et al., 2010).

Within marine environments, bioluminescence is found across a remarkable diversity of taxa, spanning organisms from bacteria and planktonic protists to cnidarians, crustaceans, cephalopods, and chordates, highlighting the widespread distribution of light-producing systems throughout the marine tree of life (Haddock et al., 2010).

Marine organisms use bioluminescence for a range of ecological functions, including defence against predators, prey attraction, communication, camouflage and reproduction. The production of light plays a central role in marine ecosystems, influencing interactions between organisms throughout the water column. Because bioluminescence has evolved across numerous unrelated taxa and occurs in organisms ranging from bacteria to fish, it has become an important model for studying adaptation, convergent evolution and ecosystem dynamics in the ocean (Haddock et al., 2010).
Although bioluminescence occurs in both terrestrial and marine environments, marine organisms account for the majority of known bioluminescent diversity. This extensive taxonomic distribution makes marine bioluminescence a valuable system for examining the evolutionary processes, genetic innovations and regulatory mechanisms underlying the repeated emergence of complex biological traits (Haddock et al., 2010).

Distribution across the tree of life

Bioluminescence is distributed across a wide range of marine organisms, including bacteria, planktonic protists, cnidarians, crustaceans, cephalopods and chordates. The presence of light-producing systems within these evolutionarily distant lineages suggests that bioluminescence has arisen multiple times independently throughout evolutionary history (Haddock et al., 2010).
